# Deck AI tells: all 31, by signal strength

Source: A complete guide to making your decks not AI (https://aa-not-ai-decks.vercel.app/not-ai-decks-guide.md, Analyst Academy, November 2026). Numbers are per the guide; tell numbers match the guide's, so they can be looked up there.

## How the comparison was measured
Read: https://aa-not-ai-decks.vercel.app/not-ai-decks-guide.md#:~:text=What%20100%20AI%20vs%20100%20real%20slides%20showed
- **AI set**: 94 slides from 720p frames of Paul Moss's video comparing 17 AI presentation tools.
- **Real set**: 100 slides sampled stratified-randomly from SlideStart (23 consulting firms, banks and research firms; 90 published decks; no cover slides).
- **Scoring**: one person, same rubric, not blind to the set. "AI vs real" below is the share of slides with the tell. Some rates are only over slides that had an image, chart, cards or numbers; those are marked.
- **Reading the tiers**: strong means the tell separates the sets; weak means real decks do it too, so it won't give a deck away alone, though the fix may still improve the slide.

## Strong signal
Read: https://aa-not-ai-decks.vercel.app/not-ai-decks-guide.md#:~:text=The%20visual%20tells

**19. Anything above the title (46% vs 8%)**, the strongest tell. A small letter-spaced ALL-CAPS label ("KEY INSIGHT", "01 / THE PROBLEM"), a sentence-case kicker, a breadcrumb or a pill sitting over the real title, usually repeating it. Real decks that do it use a section name or "Appendix". Fix: open with the title. If you need section markers, put them in the template once, small, same place every slide.

**29. Production slips (30% vs 7%, in 12 of 17 tools)**, the most tool-independent tell. Text running into a chart, image or other text; labels spilling out of a box or off the slide; leftover "Click to add title", "[Detail]", "xxx", "Series 1"; words split by wrapping; title numbers that disagree with the chart. Fix: view every slide at full size and fix each one; have the tool list its leftover placeholders, then check yourself anyway.

**12. Same-shape sentences (40% vs 19%)**. Colon titles ("Growth: The Path Forward"), an em-dash pivot in every line, every sentence the same length. Fix: short, active sentences you'd say aloud to a colleague; vary the length.

**30. Takeaway box or bottom banner (18% vs 3%)**. A panel labelled "Key takeaway", "So what", "Bottom line", or a filled strip along the bottom restating the slide. Fix: the takeaway goes in the title; a remaining so-what line is plain body text with no box, fill or label.

**31. Full-height coloured edge strip (17% vs 1%)**. A solid bar down most of the left or right edge on every slide. Mostly from two tools, so it's a fingerprint more than a habit. Fix: remove it; keep a fixed brand edge element exactly as the template has it.

## Some signal
Read: https://aa-not-ai-decks.vercel.app/not-ai-decks-guide.md#:~:text=The%20tells%20%28and%20what%20to%20do%20instead%29

**4. Hype wording (15% vs 8%)**. Unlock, elevate, delve, dive in, leverage, empower, harness, seamless, robust, game-changer, cutting-edge, transformative, synergy, journey, "in today's fast-paced world", "let's explore". Fix: name the product, team, number, date or place; "cut onboarding from three weeks to one".

**9. Centred everything (6% vs 0%)**. Centred titles, bullets, paragraphs and images on every slide. Fix: left-align body text on a consistent grid; centre only a single number, a one-line quote or a section break.

**13. Inflated significance (3% vs 0%)**. "A pivotal moment", "a testament to", "plays a crucial role", "part of a broader trend". Fix: say what happened and how big it was; if the size is unknown, write `[DATA NEEDED: ...]`.

**17. Groups of three by habit (21% vs 11%)**. "Fast, simple and powerful"; three bullets whether or not there are three things. Fix: as many items as there are; the vaguest of three is usually filler.

## Weak signal, similar in both sets
Read: https://aa-not-ai-decks.vercel.app/not-ai-decks-guide.md#:~:text=The%20tells%20%28and%20what%20to%20do%20instead%29

These won't give a deck away alone. The first four are about substance and worth fixing for their own sake.

**8. Unsourced numbers (80% vs 53%, slides with numbers)**. Round figures, "73% of executives agree", plausible made-up chart data. Fix: every number has a source you could name; otherwise `[DATA NEEDED: ...]`. Never let the tool fill the gap.

**14. Vague attribution (0% vs 0%)**. "Experts say", "studies show", "many believe". Fix: name the person, report or dataset with a date, or cut the claim; mark `[SOURCE NEEDED: ...]`.

**7. Uniform bullet walls (21% vs 17%)**. Five to seven same-length bullets on every slide. Fix: one idea per slide; split a seven-bullet slide; vary the rhythm (a number, a chart, a short list).

**22. Everything the same weight (16% vs 17%)**. Equal boxes and fonts, headline barely bigger than body, nothing wins. Fix: pick the one thing the eye lands on first and make it dominant; demote the rest.

**3. Purple glow and glassmorphism (6% vs 8%)**. Navy-to-violet gradients, glowing orbs, frosted cards, gradient text. Fix: flat fills and a deliberate palette (brand colours, or one background, one text colour, one grey, one accent).

**26. The newer default palettes (7% vs 8%)**. Cream with a serif title and terracotta accent; dark navy or black with one bright accent; dark blue-to-orange. Fix: choose the palette on purpose, or use the brand's.

**5. Symmetric three-box grid (11% vs 8%; three identical boxes 6% vs 3%)**. Fix: asymmetric layouts that follow the content; make the item that matters most bigger.

**6. Stock 3D blobs and unrelated hero images (94% vs 57%, slides with images)**. Fix: show the real product, screenshot, chart or place, or no image.

**16. "It's not just X, it's Y" (1% vs 1%)**. Fix: delete the first half and make Y specific.

**18. Rounded box with a coloured accent edge (2% vs 0%)**. Rare per slide, but one AI deck repeated it on 18 of 28 slides, and Paul Moss calls it one of the most obvious tells when repeated. Fix: separate with whitespace or a thin grey rule; delete the box if the slide is as clear without it.

**20. Accent bar above or rule under the title (12% vs 8%)**. Some real decks use it as house style. Fix: drop the tool-added bar; keep a template's fixed graphic as designed.

**28. Row of dashboard stat tiles (9% vs 7%)**. Four to six equal big-number tiles, inviting made-up fillers. Fix: show the one number that proves the title, large or as a chart; the rest goes in a table or appendix.

## More common in real decks
Read: https://aa-not-ai-decks.vercel.app/not-ai-decks-guide.md#:~:text=The%20visual%20tells

Removing these does not make a deck look less AI-made. Two still matter for quality (1 and 11); the rest are style calls for the brand or the designer.

**1. Generic titles (30% vs 43%)**. "Overview", "Key Insights", "Next Steps". Still the most useful fix in the guide: one full sentence saying what the slide proves ("Q3 revenue grew, but almost all of it came from two accounts"). Test: the titles alone carry the argument.

**11. Chart junk and decorative diagrams (39% vs 49%, slides with charts)**. 3D pies, distant legends, every gridline, a cycle diagram for a process that isn't one. Fix: takeaway title, chart, an annotation on the point; grey for context, accent for the point.

**25. Accent colour everywhere (18% vs 51%)**. Change only if nothing stands out any more: keep the accent for the one thing the slide proves.

**2. Emoji bullets and icons in circles (18% vs 34%)**. Change only when the icon repeats the word next to it and teaches nothing.

**23. Default type (17% vs 38%)**. Standard fonts are fine. What still hurts: many sizes in play, emphasis in bold and colour and size at once, a title whose last words switch to italic serif or another colour. Fix: one typeface, regular and bold, three or four sizes.

**24. Floating cards and drop shadows (7% vs 17%, slides with cards)**. Watch for cards inside cards; otherwise a house card style is fine.

**27. Pills, badges and status dots (13% vs 18%)**. Change only when the badge is decoration posing as information; write the status in the cell or sentence instead.

## Not measured
Read: https://aa-not-ai-decks.vercel.app/not-ai-decks-guide.md#:~:text=The%20tells%20%28and%20what%20to%20do%20instead%29

**10. Same layout on every slide** (deck-level). Fix: rotate a few layouts by job (statement, chart, comparison); consistency comes from fonts, colours and margins.

**15. The tidy future-outlook ending** (closing slides weren't compared). "Challenges and Future Prospects", "Despite these challenges...". Fix: end on the decision or the ask, with a date; put risks next to the decision they affect.

**21. Full-sentence subtitle on the cover** (no cover slides in the real set). Fix: a few words (audience, meeting, date), or none.
