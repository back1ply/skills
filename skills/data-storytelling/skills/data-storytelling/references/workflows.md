# Workflows: the user's side of the conversation

Adapted from the conversation starters in the *SWD + AI Primer*. When the user's request matches a stage, run that stage. When the user is unsure where to start, offer these as next steps.

| Stage | User brings | You do |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **1. Start with context** | Audience, what they care about, stakes, desired outcome | Pressure-test it: what is being overlooked or assumed? Ask clarifying questions, then help write a one-sentence **Big Idea** (point of view + what's at stake). |
| **2. Craft a story** | Brainstormed ideas, storyboard, or planned content | Find gaps, detours, and places where momentum drops. Check that there's real tension and that the ending resolves it. Propose a reordering if the flow is unclear. |
| **3. Choose a visual** | Data + the takeaway | Show 2–3 viable approaches (render them if you can) and explain what each makes easy to see and what it hides. |
| **4. Design graphs and slides** | A draft graph or slide | Critique it for clarity: what to remove or simplify, whether attention lands on what matters, where words, hierarchy, or design could be clearer. **Remove before you add.** |
| **5. Present powerfully** | Audience, key message, planned points | Find where the message might be unclear or fail to land. List the hardest questions this audience will likely ask and what else to prepare for. |

## Keep iterating

The first response is a starting point. Encourage the user to say what they agree or disagree with, ask "why?", request alternatives, add context, try an idea, and bring the result back for another critique. Their judgment decides what happens next.

## Data sensitivity

Remind users to follow their organization's policy before pasting sensitive data. Often an anonymized or aggregated sample is enough for a design critique.
