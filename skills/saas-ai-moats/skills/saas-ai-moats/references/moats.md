# SaaS moats: the 8 that still work

Source: The Only SaaS Moats Left in 2026 (youtube.com/watch?v=KpILHX2D-AA, 2026-10-18). Details are as the video describes them. Company examples and figures are from the video (2026).

The test for every moat: assume a competitor can clone your code for nearly nothing. What still stops customers from switching? Score each moat from 0 to 100.

## Why this matters even if you never sell

Watch: [0:17:02](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1022)

Private equity does about 70% of acquisitions of bootstrapped-size SaaS companies, per the video, and it follows public-market multiples. Buyers aren't worried about this year. They're worried whether the business exists in 1, 3 or 5 years. Moats are what make them pay the old multiples. Public-market signal from the video: HubSpot beat earnings and still dropped about 21%, while Atlassian rose about 35% as its MCP server usage doubled. Jira has several of these moats, and HubSpot's inbound engine is exposed to AI ([0:18:02](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1082)).

## 1. Buyers know and trust you

Watch: [0:19:03](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1143)
- **What**: a strong brand, knowing your buyers and how they buy, partners whose businesses are built around recommending you, and an internal champion who fights for you. At full strength, a competitor with better, vibe-coded software still has to build all of this.
- **Example**: QuickBooks. "Terrible software", but accountants recommend it, so a better product never gets into the conversation (Xero is still behind after 10+ years). Shopify: thousands of implementation agencies have already picked the platform for the merchant ([0:20:04](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1204)).
- **Ask**: who has built their business or career around recommending or defending you?
- **First action**: list your partner and referral channels by name. If the list is empty, this moat is near 0.

## 2. You are the system of record

Watch: [0:21:05](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1265)
- **What**: the business's source of truth, with daily operations built around you: balances, approvals, statuses, workflows, configured roles and rules, and other systems reading from you or writing into you. The strongest form is being the business's operating system.
- **Example**: Jira. Tickets export in an afternoon, but history, audit trail, integrations and moving several teams don't ([0:22:05](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1325)). Gymdesk (a TinySeed company) grew from one gym feature into billing, invoicing and door-fob hardware. It runs the whole gym.
- **Ask**: if a customer wants to leave, what can't they export, what breaks downstream, and what would they have to rebuild? ([0:23:06](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1386))
- **First action**: find the next adjacent workflow your customers run outside your product, and take it over.

## 3. You're allowed to act

Watch: [0:23:06](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1386)
- **What**: the product performs the action instead of advising. A tool that tells a clerk whom to pay is a report. A tool that moves the money acts.
- **Example**: Stripe Connect actually moves money. Reimbi (sp?), a TinySeed company, reimburses candidates and other non-vendors via Venmo, PayPal or Cash App from start to finish. Churnkey sits in the cancellation flow and makes retention offers without anyone checking, where most churn tools only predict ([0:24:00](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1440), [0:25:01](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1501)).
- **Ask**: can your software act, or only advise?
- **First action**: find the recommendation your users act on most often, and get the permission to perform it.

## 4. They don't check your work

Watch: [0:25:01](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1501)
- **What**: customers trust the output so fully that they never double-check it. You earn this by being right for years, not just by being trustworthy.
- **Example**: Gusto (payroll). He has used it since about 2013 and never checked anything beyond his W-2, so a vibe-coded rival won't win him. Avalara (sales tax): nobody checks the number ([0:26:02](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1562)). He couldn't find a small-company example.
- **Ask**: do customers trust your output enough not to double-check it?
- **First action**: measure how often users edit or override your output, and drive that toward zero.

## 5. Someone official already said yes

Watch: [0:26:02](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1562)
- **What**: regulations, licenses, accreditations and certifications where a third-party body has approved *you, the vendor*. Serving regulated customers doesn't count ([0:27:03](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1623)).
- **Example**: SOC 2, ISO 27001 and HIPAA are speed bumps, but real ones: compliance is hard enough that Vanta became a billion-dollar company. A TinySeed company sells ABA courses that count toward a credential, and a clone would need board approval. US SMS: carrier registration and consent laws are worse than email, so the code is easy and the operations are miserable ([0:28:03](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1683), [0:29:04](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1744)).
- **Ask**: what approvals, legal complexity or regulations have you navigated that a newcomer would have to earn and wait for?
- **First action**: list the certifications buyers in your vertical ask about, and start the one with the longest lead time.

## 6. Everyone's already in it

Watch: [0:29:04](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1744)
- **What**: network effect and coordination. One user can switch in an afternoon. A team, a company or a whole community has to move at once, and that's painful.
- **Example**: Slack. People try to leave and don't. Commit Swimming (a TinySeed company): swim-team software where every non-technical parent in the league is signed up for meet notifications, so the whole league would have to agree to switch ([0:30:05](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1805)).
- **Ask**: who has to agree to replace you? Would a competitor have to move everyone at once? ([0:31:07](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1867))
- **First action**: invite the next ring of people (clients, parents, vendors) into the product as participants.

## 7. Every customer makes it smarter

Watch: [0:31:07](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1867)
- **What**: a learning loop. Something happens, a person or the software acts, you see the result, and you correct the system. Having lots of data isn't enough. You must use it to predict across all customers.
- **Three gates. All must pass or it doesn't count** ([0:33:01](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1981)):
  1. Every customer makes it better for every *other* customer, not only for themselves.
  2. Customers can take their data, but not what you learned from it.
  3. It keeps learning. A stagnant model isn't a moat.
- **Example**: Stripe Radar (fraud patterns across all merchants; no single merchant could build it). Ramp (one customer's line-item correction helps everyone). Mailchimp's Omnivore spam engine (around 2009), the reason freemium worked for them. Churnkey *could* have one if every cancellation trained its offers ([0:32:00](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1920)).
- **Ask**: does what happens to one customer make the product smarter for the rest?
- **First action**: pick one decision your product makes for all customers, and feed the pooled outcomes back into it.

## 8. It's hard to keep running

Watch: [0:33:01](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=1981)
- **What**: easy to build, painful to operate: DevOps, servers, rotating IPs, constant fighting.
- **Example**: Pieter Levels, who vibe-codes nearly everything, tried for weeks to replace ScrapingBee and gave up. It's one of the few things he still pays for, next to Cloudflare and bare servers. ScrapingBee later sold (an eight-figure exit, per the video). Drip: anyone can send email, but they had full-time staff doing nothing but fighting spam. Vibe-coded "bring your own Amazon SES" email apps miss this ([0:34:02](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=2042), [0:35:05](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=2105)).
- **Ask**: is it hard to build, or hard to keep running? What do you maintain that you resent maintaining?
- **First action**: before automating away or sunsetting a painful ops burden, ask whether it's the reason customers can't leave.

## Moves: how to get a moat you don't have

Watch: [0:37:08](https://youtu.be/KpILHX2D-AA?t=2228)

| If you are… | Move toward… | Moat |
|---|---|---|
| A vertical tool | An ecosystem; the vendor the industry recommends | 1 |
| A point solution | System of record | 2 |
| A reporting tool | Taking the action (move the money, run the save flow) | 3 |
| A calculator | The output nobody double-checks | 4 |
| In a regulated industry | The approved provider | 5 |
| A single-seat tool | Network effects; everyone coordinating in it | 6 |
| A data collector | A learning loop | 7 |
| Babysitting infrastructure you hate | A burden nobody else will carry | 8 |
